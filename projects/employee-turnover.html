Employee Turnover Prediction

An analysis of employee turnover patterns and a test of whether HR data could help guide retention conversations at fictional Salifort Motors.

OverviewBusiness problemObjectiveApproachToolsFindingsRecommendationsEvidence

Overview

This practice project uses a public Kaggle HR dataset as part of the Google certificate. Salifort Motors is fictional. The dataset had 14,999 rows; 3,008 duplicates (20%) were removed, leaving 11,991 employees. 16.6% had left.

14,999

Business problem

Leadership at fictional Salifort Motors wanted to understand employee turnover and whether available HR data could help identify patterns linked with leaving. The analysis considered workload, satisfaction, tenure, promotion, evaluation, and department.

Objective

Explore patterns associated with attrition, compare a logistic regression baseline with a tree-based model, and identify practical questions for retention policies while treating predictions cautiously.

Approach

  1. Removed duplicate records and explored how turnover related to workload, satisfaction, tenure, promotion, evaluation, and department.
  2. Fit a logistic regression model as a baseline.
  3. For the random forest, removed satisfaction level as a likely leakage feature and engineered an “overworked” flag for employees working more than 175 hours per month.
  4. Compared model performance on the test data and reviewed the most important features.

Tools

  • Python
  • Logistic regression
  • Random forest
  • Feature engineering

Findings

  • All 145 employees with seven projects left.
  • Leavers fell into two workload groups: employees who were underworked relative to peers and employees who were heavily overworked.
  • Employees around the four-year mark showed notably low satisfaction. The longest-tenured employees did not leave, and promotion in the last five years was associated with staying.
  • Department made little difference to the turnover patterns.
  • The most important random forest features were last evaluation, number of projects, tenure, and the overworked flag.

Model results

Logistic regression identified 26% of leavers (recall 0.26; precision 0.44). The random forest, after the feature changes described above, scored as follows on the test data:

High scores are common on this dataset. Model predictions should support HR conversations, not individual employment decisions.

Recommendations

  • Review project assignments and consider a cap on the number of projects per employee.
  • Investigate dissatisfaction around the four-year mark and consider whether promotion paths are clear.
  • Clarify overtime expectations and pay policy.
  • Avoid tying evaluation to long hours.

These are review topics suggested by the observed patterns, not claims of guaranteed effects.
